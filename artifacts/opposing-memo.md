# Opposing memorandum: do not manufacture a collateral constraint

**Status:** Independent self-challenge; not external review  
**Case version:** MARA-CR-001  
**Alternative recommendation:** Decline the fictional secured structure pending real diligence; if diligence is satisfactory, consider the full $5.0 million on an unsecured or borrowing-base structure tied to actual repayment capacity.

> MARA is a public-information case. All facility, collateral, Base, portfolio, policy, and rating terms are fictional. No Coinbase relationship is asserted.

The $3.0 million recommendation in the [credit memo](credit-memo.md) is precise but not yet persuasive. Its binding cap is generated by an invented 4.0 million USDC collateral lot and invented costs (ASM-C002, ASM-C003). The cap therefore demonstrates a method; it does not establish an economically appropriate limit for MARA.

Public facts point in two directions. MARA reported $421.3 million of cash and cash equivalents and 26,307 unrestricted bitcoin at June 30, 2026 (SRC-001). A $5.0 million request equals about 1.2% of reported cash, before considering bitcoin. On scale alone, reducing the request by $2.0 million because an invented collateral lot supports only $3.046 million is artificial.

The same filing also reports approximately $2.4 billion of debt, substantial near-term obligations, and cash designated for a majority-owned subsidiary; the 2025 Form 10-K reports $802.7 million of operating cash use (SRC-001, SRC-002). Those are reasons to demand private diligence, not reasons to let fictional collateral substitute for a primary repayment analysis.

The defensible choice today is therefore **no approval on the present record**. The lender should first obtain the facility purpose, borrowing-entity financials, 13-week cash forecast, legal-entity cash map, complete debt-service schedule, and actual proposed collateral terms. If those materials establish repayment capacity, the committee should assess the full $5.0 million directly. If they do not, a $3.0 million secured exposure is not automatically safe.

This alternative is stronger because it avoids two false signals:

1. that a lower number is conservative even when its inputs are synthetic; and
2. that onchain collateral cures uncertainty about operating repayment, lien priority, or remedies.

The disputed assumption is ASM-C004: that a $7.5 million obligor cap is supportable from public information plus incomplete diligence. Evidence that would resolve the dispute is a reconciled cash forecast and debt-service schedule at the actual borrowing entity, with downside liquidity and covenant headroom. Until that exists, the collateral waterfall should be presented as an illustrative sensitivity only, not the basis for an approval.
